# The Cartographer's Letter

Mira unfolded the map on the kitchen table, smoothing the creases with the heel of her hand. It was older than her — older than her mother, even — a chart of a coast that had not existed in anyone's lifetime. Her grandmother had drawn it during the last year of the war, when the shoreline was retreating a meter at a time and the names of the towns kept changing.

"Everything that has a name can be lost," her grandmother used to say. "So we name carefully."

# The Names on the Margin

Mira was twelve the first time she noticed the names written in tiny capitals along the margin: ELARA, TAVIN, IOR. She asked her grandmother what they meant.

"Places that wanted to be remembered," she said. "I gave them names before the sea could take them away."

# A Letter Never Sent

After her grandmother died, Mira found the letter. It was addressed to no one she knew, and dated the week before the surrender. The handwriting was steady, the language careful, but the last line was different — hurried, almost frantic.

*Find the coast that is left, and stand where I stood. The names are still there. They are waiting.*

Mira read it again and again. She did not understand it then. She would not understand it for another twenty years.
